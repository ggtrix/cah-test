Chapter 1 · The findings
02  ·  Why now

Facing a threat baseline that is getting increasingly complex.

Critical access is a Medicare designation for hospitals serving sparsely populated communities, where the financial challenges of inpatient care also limit security budgets.

The designation

What it requires

No more than 25 inpatient beds, an average acute length of stay of 96 hours or less, 24-hour emergency services, and a qualifying distance from the next hospital — in exchange for cost-based reimbursement.

The consequence

No local alternative

When one of these hospitals goes offline, care does not shift to a competitor across town; it shifts to a facility a long ambulance ride away. Downtime tolerance here is materially lower than the healthcare average implies.

The exposure

An expanding attack surface

Patient portals, telehealth endpoints, remote monitoring, imaging archives and revenue-cycle vendors all terminate on infrastructure carrying the hospital's name — whether or not anyone is maintaining an inventory of it.

Ransomware attacks shifted from opportunistic encryption to deliberate targeting of care delivery, on the assumption that a hospital cannot tolerate downtime and will negotiate quickly. The regulatory floor is rising in the same period: the HIPAA Security Rule update proposed in January 2025 would remove the "addressable" classification and mandate vulnerability scanning every six months, annual penetration testing and a complete asset inventory (final rule scheduled July 2027) — the practices this report measures.

Why this report

These findings required no hospital involvement and minimal cost to uncover. Insurers, plaintiffs' experts and attackers can access the same information. This report is provided to help guide hospitals on how to improve their overall cyber resilience.

The KEV figure needs a caveat, and it is a big one

23.4% of domains carry a CISA KEV match, but only 13 distinct KEV identifiers appear across the entire sector — and one of them, CVE-2020-11023 (a JQuery cross-site-scripting flaw), accounts for 84.3% of the 229 affected domains on its own. That is a real cataloged vulnerability worth remediating, but a client-side library issue, not a server compromise path. The KEVs ransomware attackers actually use are present on 18 domains — 1.8% of the sector.

What this means in plain English: Treat "23.4% carry a KEV" as a basic patch-hygiene signal, and the 1.8% figure as the real ransomware-exposure signal. Quoting the first number without the second makes the sector look worse than it is.

CMS launched the Rural Health Transformation Program on 29 December 2025 under Public Law 119-21: $50 billion over five years, half distributed equally among states with approved applications and half allocated on rurality metrics, state policy actions and assessed application impact, with cybersecurity named among the uses of funds. A state that can put a measured exposure baseline into its application makes a materially more concrete case than one describing its rural cyber resilience in general terms — evidence of exposure has become a funding asset.

08  ·  Six configuration moves

Six changes close most of the gap.

Six changes account for the large majority of the exposure measured in this report. None requires a security product; all are within reach of a hospital with a small IT team and an accommodating managed-service provider.

The six moves
#Configuration moveSector exposure closedEffortTimeWhat to do
01Move DMARC to p=reject62.2%Low2–6 weeksPublish a DMARC record, monitor the aggregate reports for a fortnight to catch every legitimate sender, then step to quarantine and then to reject.
02Tighten SPF to -all51.0%Low1–2 weeksEnumerate every legitimate sending service — EHR, billing, scheduling, imaging — then replace the trailing ~all or ?all with -all.
03Remove abandoned subdomains45.4%LowDaysExport the full DNS zone, resolve every record, and delete those pointing at services that no longer answer. The highest impact-to-effort action in this report.
04Allowlist admin and database ports65.3%Medium2–4 weeksFTP, SSH, RDP, MySQL and PostgreSQL should not answer to the open internet. Restrict to a VPN or source-IP allowlist, and retire FTP in favor of SFTP.
05Patch the known-exploited set first23.4%Medium30–90 daysWork the CISA KEV catalog ahead of the general CVE backlog, then EPSS above 0.5, ransomware-linked and server-side first.
06Build and keep an asset inventory—Medium30 days to draftEnumerate every hostname that resolves to the organization, assign a named owner, review quarterly. Every other control depends on this one.

Run these on a dated plan: form a working group in week 1 (IT lead, compliance officer and the managed-service provider, reporting to the CEO), close the three low-effort DNS and email changes inside the first fortnight, work the KEV remediation sprint through day 30, lock down administrative ports by day 60, and move DMARC to reject with the asset inventory complete and an external re-scan by day 90.

09  ·  Methodology, sources, notices

How this report was constructed.

Population and scope

The master registry holds 5,432 CMS-certified hospital facilities, of which 1,378 carry the critical access designation. Those map to 1,009 distinct domains; scan data exists for 977 of them (96.8%), covering 1,697 facilities and 15,037 internet-facing subdomains de-duplicated to 7,467 — the population of this report. A domain enters the study if at least one critical access hospital sits behind it, and every percentage is stated against the 977-domain count unless another denominator is given.

Data sources and method

Findings come from passive and lightly active external observation only: DNS and certificate transparency records, banner and service fingerprinting, published email-authentication records, and HTTP response analysis. No system was authenticated to and no intrusive test was performed. Vulnerability enrichment comes from the CISA KEV catalog (1,699 entries as at 2026-09-08), FIRST's EPSS model (scores as at 2026-09-08), and the CVE Program record for each identifier.

Score construction

Each subdomain is scored across five pillars — Threat & Exploit Intelligence 30%, External Attack Surface 25%, Infrastructure Security 20%, Web Application Security 15%, Email Security 10%. The subdomain composite is 0.6 × the highest pillar + 0.4 × the weighted average, floored at (highest pillar − 10) where the highest pillar is at or above 75. The hospital's headline is its worst subdomain. Bands: Critical ≥ 80, High ≥ 45, Medium ≥ 25, Low ≥ 10, Minimal below 10.

Limitations, stated plainly

01

Scan vintage is not uniform

The 977 domains come from three scan runs, with the newest data used for each domain. Domains from the most recent batch score higher on average; the direction of that bias is unknown and this study does not correct for it.

02

External exposure is not the whole risk picture

Nothing here observes internal segmentation, backup integrity, endpoint protection, staff training or vendor risk. A hospital can score well here and still be poorly defended internally.

03

Fingerprinting infers versions, it does not confirm them

A CVE match means a subdomain presented an artifact consistent with a vulnerable version. Back-ported patches and compensating controls are invisible from outside, so a proportion will be false positives on inspection.

04

Coverage is high but not complete

32 critical access domains have no scan data in any source and are absent from every figure here. They are not assumed safe; they are simply not measured.

05

The KEV headline is concentrated

As set out in section 03, one jQuery identifier accounts for 84.3% of KEV matches. Any use of the 23.4% KEV figure without that context will mislead.

The bottom line

The exposure is real. The fixes are cheap. Inaction is what costs.

Four of the six changes in section 08 are configuration work that can be closed inside a single quarter without procurement: DMARC to reject, SPF to -all, abandoned subdomains removed, administrative ports closed. If a re-scan ninety days later shows no movement, the constraint was never budget.
